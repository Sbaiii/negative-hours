# Glossary

**Day-ahead market** — auction held each day at noon (CET) setting prices for every period of the next day. Our main price series.

**Bidding zone** — the geographic area with a single wholesale price. Usually a country; Germany and Luxembourg share one (`DE_LU`); Italy and the Nordics are split into several.

**MTU (Market Time Unit)** — length of one price period. Was 60 minutes; the European day-ahead market moved to 15 minutes in 2025.

**Negative price** — the price is below €0/MWh: producers pay to deliver power. Happens when inflexible or subsidised generation exceeds demand.

**Capture price** — the average price a technology actually earns, weighted by when it produces. Solar capture price = Σ(price × solar output) / Σ(solar output).

**Capture rate** — capture price ÷ average baseload price. Below 1 = the technology earns less than the average hour.

**Cannibalisation** — the more solar is installed, the lower midday prices fall, so each new panel earns less.

**Battery arbitrage** — charge when cheap, discharge when expensive. Simplest model: one cycle/day, buy the cheapest window, sell the most expensive, minus efficiency losses (~85–90% round-trip).

**EUR/MWh** — euros per megawatt-hour. €100/MWh = €0.10/kWh wholesale.
